What we can and can't conclude yet

  • Self-efficacy: the estimate (+0.07) is smaller than this sample can reliably detect (about ±0.14). We can't yet tell a small benefit from none.
  • Uneven dropout: 12-month response differs by 14% between groups. Estimates are weighted for who stayed, but if those who left differ in ways we didn't measure, the results could be biased. Improve follow-up with comparison youth (e.g., incentives, school-based surveying).
  • Missing-data check: weighted and complete-case estimates agree within 0.05 SD for every outcome.
  • Weak scales: Bridging ties, Supportive adults hold together poorly (Cronbach's alpha < 0.70). Report their items separately or revise the items.
  • Sample size: about 400 youth per group are needed for 80% power to detect a 0.2 SD effect (the typical size in mentoring research); a 0.1 SD effect needs more than 600 per group.
  • Not answered by these data: long-term outcomes (graduation, college, earnings), whether effects last after the match ends, and which program components matter most. Relationship-quality and dose comparisons are observational.

Method. Lottery-assigned comparison group (waitlist). ANCOVA of each 12-month scale score on group, baseline score and site; inverse-probability-of-retention weights; robust standard errors; Hedges' g using the baseline standard deviation. Secondary: mixed-effects growth models. Subgroup and dose analyses are exploratory.

Privacy. Pseudonymous IDs only; names and goal text are held separately by program staff; groups under 20 suppressed. People, not this dashboard, make decisions about participants and the program.
